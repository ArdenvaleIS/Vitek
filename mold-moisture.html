You paid to have the mold removed. Now it is back, and you are being asked to pay again. Cleaning can remove growth that has already developed, but it does not necessarily correct the moisture conditions that allowed it to develop.

If the mold returned, removal may not have addressed the moisture source

Remediation is sometimes necessary. Contaminated materials may need to be removed, and affected areas cleaned and dried. Those steps address existing growth. Preventing recurrence also requires understanding and correcting the conditions that allowed moisture to accumulate.

This is not necessarily a criticism of previous work. Mold removal and building-moisture investigation are related but different tasks, and a project may not have included a full review of the building conditions.

My focus is the underlying building conditions.

I do not sell mold treatments, insulation products or replacement equipment. I provide independent observations, technical assessment and practical recommendations.

Moisture is the condition to control

Mold spores are common in indoor and outdoor environments. A building cannot realistically be made permanently “mold-free.” The practical objective is to control moisture, correct water problems, and address mold growth appropriately.

Moisture may be present in the air, crawl space, wall cavity or attic. The issue is not simply whether moisture exists, but whether water accumulates or humid air reaches surfaces cold enough for condensation. Persistent dampness can support mold growth and material damage.

Think of the house as a fortress: the best defense is to keep unwanted water outside. Equipment that removes water after it has entered a crawl space can help manage the condition, but it does not necessarily stop the water from entering.

The three moisture mechanisms I look for

These mechanisms can occur separately or interact with one another. Identifying which ones are present is more useful than assuming every mold problem has the same cause.

1 · Water entry

Water entering the building

Roof or flashing leaks, plumbing leaks, window or exterior-wall leakage, poor drainage, groundwater, or soil moisture reaching a crawl space can introduce liquid water.

If water continues to enter, drying the area repeatedly may not solve the source.

2 · Air leakage

Air moving through the building envelope

Air can move through gaps, penetrations and poorly sealed assemblies. Depending on conditions, that air can carry moisture into colder parts of a wall, attic or crawl space.

Air sealing must be considered together with ventilation and moisture management for the particular assembly.

3 · Cold surfaces

Surfaces cold enough for condensation

When humid air contacts a surface below its dew-point temperature, water can condense. Window glass after a shower is a familiar example; similar conditions can occur at thermal bridges or poorly insulated areas.

Persistent condensation can keep materials damp.

The insulation trap: good material does not guarantee a good assembly

Insulation can help keep interior surfaces warmer and reduce heat transfer. But insulation alone does not solve every moisture problem.

Gaps, voids, compression, missing sections or poorly detailed transitions can leave colder areas. The result depends not only on the insulation material, but also on how it works with air sealing, water control, vapor control and ventilation in that particular wall, roof or floor assembly.

A product such as mineral wool (including Rockwool) may be appropriate in many applications, but choosing a good material does not compensate for an unsuitable design or installation.

What an independent assessment can cover

What I can review

  • Visual observations of accessible areas and visible moisture-related conditions
  • Potential water-entry points, drainage and crawl-space moisture conditions
  • Indoor humidity, temperature and surface-moisture readings where appropriate
  • Air leakage, ventilation, HVAC operation and possible condensation conditions
  • Insulation continuity and visible thermal-bridge conditions where accessible
  • An existing or proposed remediation, insulation or ventilation scope
  • Photographic documentation and a written summary of findings and prioritized recommendations

What I do not provide

  • Mold removal, cleaning, remediation or reconstruction
  • Laboratory analysis, mold-species identification or medical assessment
  • Opening walls or other invasive investigation
  • Plumbing, roofing, electrical, HVAC or other licensed trade repairs
  • Engineering designs or services reserved for a licensed professional
  • A guarantee that mold will never return

If the situation requires specialized testing, invasive investigation, remediation, structural evaluation or medical advice, I will explain that and recommend using an appropriately qualified professional.

Common questions

Should I stop remediation that is already under way?

Do not stop necessary remediation based only on an online description or a preliminary consultation. Remediation professionals should address immediate contamination and safety needs. I can separately review the building moisture conditions and the proposed scope when that is useful.

Can you guarantee the mold will not come back?

No. I can assess accessible conditions, identify likely contributing mechanisms and provide recommendations. Outcomes depend on the actual source, access to concealed areas, the work performed and future building conditions. No responsible visual assessment can guarantee that mold will never return.

Do you test for mold?

I do not provide mold sampling, laboratory analysis or mold-species identification. When visible mold is present, identifying and correcting the moisture problem is usually the more useful starting point. If specialized sampling is warranted, it should be designed and interpreted by a qualified professional.

Are you working for my contractor?

No. I provide independent consulting directly to you. I do not sell remediation products, insulation or equipment, and I do not receive commissions or referral fees from contractors or vendors.

Will you tell me to replace all the insulation?

Not automatically. Insulation may be part of the issue, but water entry, air leakage, ventilation and surface temperatures must also be considered. Recommendations depend on the conditions found.
